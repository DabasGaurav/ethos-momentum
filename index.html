The rules, in the order the engine runs them

  1. Remove our errors. A lapse whose failed payment our billing log ties to a wrong-date charge leaves the agent's record and its clawback is refunded. Billing operations owns the log; an agent can contest a lapse with the payment record, decided within 14 days.
  2. Replacements reviewed, charged to whoever made them. A replacement needs a signed side-by-side coverage comparison and earns an advance only on extra premium. Replacement rates are tracked separately; one that review finds didn't benefit the customer counts as a lapse for the agent who made it, never the original seller.
  3. Mature evidence only. A persistency window (4 months = early signal; 7 and 13 months = verdict) counts only if at least of the agent's policies are that old. If no window qualifies, the agent is new and starts Capped.
  4. Compare with peers selling similar policies to similar customers (product × premium band × customer income band). If a segment has too few peers, the engine uses the next-broader one.
  5. Too big to be luck. A gap counts only above 2 standard errors (z > 2), so a few bad weeks can't move anyone.
  6. Tier. Significantly worse at the 7- or 13-month verdict and lapsing at least 2× peers: As-paid. Significantly worse at any window, or carrying unpaid debt: Capped. In line or better at every window with no debt: Full. Worse but possibly luck: no change. Down can jump; up moves one step at a time.
  7. Limit, in advance dollars. Full: up to of unearned advances. Capped: plus the advance on every customer who has paid past 4 months, up to $30,000. As-paid: paid as customers pay.
  8. Agency ceiling. An agency's agents together can't hold more unearned advances than the agency's book of customers paying past 4 months supports, plus a starting allowance for a new agency. Existing debt counts against it; the allowance stays as a floor only while that debt is repaid on schedule. Above it, new sales are paid as customers pay until payments lift it.
  9. Debt and pauses. Debt is recovered at no more than a third of each payment. New business pauses only for suspected fraud or repeated harmful replacements, while under review.

All thresholds are illustrative, to be calibrated against actual losses. Every number in Situations and Summit is computed from these rules and the inputs.
